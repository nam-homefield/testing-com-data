02

The most common way a cart dies is walking back to the pricing page

Revenue 130 cart sessions

Of 130 US sessions that added to cart, 36 ended sitting on the checkout page and 25 went back out to /std-test-pricing/. Those mean different things. Stalling on checkout is a form or payment problem; returning to pricing after seeing the total reads as price reaction. Worth watching next to the in-home collection fee, which nearly doubles some orders.

Use the orders report for the actual conversion rate, not this. Clarity samples, and it splits one shopper across several session records, so any funnel percentage from it understates. Aug 19 was 61 carts and 6 orders on the Stripe side.

03

“GET RESULTS FAST” is the most dead-clicked thing on the site

Quick fix 14 dead clicks 3 pages

Dead-clicked on the homepage, /tests/ and /popular-lab-tests/. Checked live at origin: it is a <p class="how-it-works-step-title"> in the How It Works explainer, not a link and never was.

People read the three steps as three things they can do. Either link the step cards or stop styling them like affordances. Same on “My Cart Your cart is empty”, dead-clicked three times.

04

Every checkout entry loads the page twice

Technical 188 page loads

The lowercase order URL loads first and sits for about a second, then the uppercase one loads. A case redirect on the highest-value page on the site, paid on every order. 26 of the recorded loads are that throwaway first hop.

Speed itself looks fine. Largest Contentful Paint runs a 1.8s median and a 3.4s 90th percentile. Ignore Clarity's performance score on this page type: 183 of 188 loads report exactly 0, which is Clarity failing to score the page rather than the page scoring badly. The five it did score averaged 83.

05

Quick-backs cluster almost entirely on the homepage

Acquisition 250 sessions

Of 250 sessions containing a quick-back, 143 entered on the homepage. Runner-up is /tests/ with 6. A quick-back is a click then an immediate return, so these are people choosing something and reversing straight out of it.

Treat this as a lead, not a result. The homepage is also the biggest entry point on the site, so some of that 143 is just traffic share. Worth a proper rate against homepage entries before acting.

06

Rage clicks are effectively absent, which changes what to watch

Method 1 session

One US rage-click session in the whole window, against 273 dead clicks in a comparable slice. Genuinely good news, but it means rage clicks are the wrong alarm to wire up here.

Any recurring monitor should track dead clicks and repeat-click gaps, and ignore rage.

Known issues with pulling data this way

Six constraints on the pull itself. Each one has already produced a wrong answer in this report before being caught.

  • Never quote a rate from Clarity. It samples, and it splits one shopper across several session records, so any funnel percentage understates. Conversion comes from the orders report.
  • 250 sessions per query, newest first, no pagination. On a busy filter that is a few hours, not a week. Widening the date range does nothing; only a narrower filter reaches further back.
  • Not every field means something on every page. The performance score reads 0 on 97% of checkout loads because Clarity cannot score that page type, not because the page is slow. Check the distribution before trusting a mean.
  • Country filters on the way in only. Inclusion-only, and the response has no per-session country to sort on later. An unfiltered first pass produced three false double-submits that vanished once India and the Philippines were dropped.
  • Masked card fields fake repeat clicks. Typing in the payment input reads as a burst of clicks on one element. Discarded here.
  • Internal QA traffic is unflagged. Known test orders from a Bellevue address place real-looking orders. Blocking those IPs in Clarity settings fixes every future pull.

What I would do next

  • Leave work item 345 alone. The duplicate-invoice fix is fully scoped and assigned; nothing here changes it.
  • Pull a wider slice on the other two patterns before filing anything: shoppers retrying after a validation bounce, and whether repeat-clicking predicts abandonment.
  • Kill the checkout case redirect so the page loads once.
  • Decide whether the How It Works steps are links; right now they are neither clearly.
  • Block internal QA IPs in Clarity so the sample stops being polluted.
  • Run the checkout and dead-click queries weekly and track the repeat-click rate as the number that matters.
