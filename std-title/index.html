Why a frozen query list and not a page count. Two obvious versions of this chart are both wrong. Counting pages by their average position blurs a page's real ranking with every marginal query it appears for, so a page ranking third for its city term lands in the 11-20 band because it also shows up at position 60 for a neighbouring city. And picking the top queries from one week and following them selects that week's flukes, whose regression to the mean reads as a decline: on this data it reported clicks down 20% where the honest number was 3%. Membership here needs a query present in all eight pre-change weeks with 30 or more impressions in each, and the list is frozen on disk so it never drifts.

Pages shown is a floor on indexed, not a count of it. Search Console only reports pages it actually surfaced. A page can be indexed and never appear in the window. The only exact source is the URL Inspection API, one call per URL against a 2,000 a day quota, which 2,034 pages would use up in a single run. Anchor links are collapsed onto their page first, since GSC counts /std-testing/alaska/#_std_testing_in_alaska separately and that adds about 400 phantom pages.

How to read this

Give it until mid-September. 2,034 pages recrawl over days to weeks, so the change reaches Google gradually. Early days are a blend of old and new titles, and Search Console finalizes on a two-day lag on top of that.

Flat is a win here. Section clicks fell 10.5% over the six weeks before this change and average position drifted from 9.2 to 10.0. The baseline was already sliding, so holding level means the change stopped a decline.

Separate CTR from position. Clicks down with position flat is a wording problem, meaning the new title is less clickable. Position down means Google thinks the page matches the query less well than it did. They need different fixes.

Clicks are not the goal. This change trades free-clinic seekers for buyers. Losing some free traffic while orders per visit rises is the change working, and a clicks-only read would call that a failure. Order volume lives on the orders report.

Check a real SERP now and then. Google rewrites titles it thinks do not match the query. If it is rewriting ours, the change never reached the user and none of these numbers are measuring it.
